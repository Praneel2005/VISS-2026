Question 1: The "Smudge vs. Erase" Dilemma (Filtering)

An image is heavily corrupted by salt-and-pepper noise (random pure white and black pixels). Student A applies a 7x7 Gaussian Blur. Student B applies a 7x7 Median Filter. Without writing any math, describe exactly what the noise will physically look like in Student A's final image versus Student B's final image.

Question 2: The "Moving Bus" Sabotage (Registration)

You are trying to stitch two photos together to make a panorama. SIFT finds 500 matching keypoints, but 50 of them are completely wrong because they attached to a bus that drove across the scene between the two shots. If you calculate the Homography using basic Least Squares on all 500 points, what will the final stitched panorama look like? Name the specific algorithm you must use to ignore the bus.

Question 3: The Crouch Test (Projective Geometry)

You take a photo looking straight down a long, flat hallway. The lines of the floor meet at a single Vanishing Point in the center of your image. Without changing the camera's angle, you physically crouch down closer to the floor and take a second photo. Does the Vanishing Point move UP, DOWN, or stay in the EXACT SAME pixel coordinate in the new image? Why?

Question 4: The Invisible Epipoles (Epipolar Geometry)

You set up a perfect stereo rig: two identical cameras placed exactly side-by-side, looking straight ahead with perfectly parallel lenses. Where exactly is the epipole located in the left camera's image? (Hint: Think about where the right camera's center would project onto the left camera's sensor).
